# Free Decision Within the Current Boundary

[English](./decide.md) | [简体中文](./decide.zh-CN.md)

> Position: an activity mechanism for accountable choice when the current boundary is provisionally sufficient. It is not a seventh or eighth dimension and does not assert a metaphysical theory of free will.

Free decision asks: **when the current facts, rules, constraints, capabilities, and values do not uniquely force one action, how can a participant form a choice that is still reasoned, attributable, revisable, and responsible for its consequences?**

"Free" here has an operational meaning: more than one action remains admissible under the current qualified boundary, or the available reasons only partially order the remaining actions. It does not mean unconstrained action and does not require proving metaphysical indeterminism.

## 1. Preconditions for deciding within the boundary

Decision within the current boundary assumes that the boundary is **provisionally sufficient for the present purpose**.

Before substantive choice, ask:

- is the problem or task framing sufficiently qualified?
- are material facts and unknowns represented with appropriate strength?
- are important candidates missing in a way that could change the choice?
- are capability and authority boundaries known well enough?
- are non-negotiable constraints, obligations, and rights represented?
- is the validity window appropriate to the rate of change?

If a missing distinction could materially change the decision and can reasonably be investigated, hand off to [exploration](./explore.md).

`boundary incomplete in principle != decision impossible`

`material boundary insufficiency != continue choosing as if the boundary were complete`

Finite action requires provisional closure, not final knowledge.

## 2. Form the admissible decision space

Let `C` be the currently distinguished candidate actions.

The current activity may narrow `C` through several independent boundaries:

- candidates that are not coherent under the current framing;
- paths whose capability remains unknown when capability must be established before action;
- paths known to be unreachable under the relevant conditions;
- transitions whose qualification conditions are unmet;
- actions outside the participant's authority;
- actions violating applicable hard constraints, rights, contracts, safety limits, or explicit commitments;
- actions whose required dependencies or recovery conditions are absent.

Call the remaining set `F`, the **currently admissible decision space**.

This is not a universal optimization formula. Different exclusions come from different semantic positions and must preserve their basis.

`not admissible because impossible != not admissible because unauthorized`

`unauthorized != valueless`

`value conflict != capability failure`

`candidate excluded != candidate disproved in every context`

If `F` is empty, the system should not fabricate a choice. It may need to change the goal, obtain capability or authority, renegotiate constraints, reopen exploration, or stop.

If `F` contains one option, the current boundary may strongly constrain the decision.

If `F` contains multiple options, the remaining choice is the primary domain of free decision in this document.

## 3. Multiple admissible options need not collapse to one optimum

Several options can remain reasonable at the same time.

They may be:

- comparable on some consequences but not others;
- tied under the currently accepted criteria;
- partially ordered rather than totally ranked;
- supported by different qualified values;
- sensitive to uncertain future conditions;
- strategically different while all remaining within authority and constraints.

Therefore:

`several admissible options != one hidden uniquely correct option must exist`

`partial comparability != inability to decide`

`no common scalar != no reasons exist`

A domain can legitimately use optimization when a qualified objective, measurement, constraints, and aggregation rule exist. The mistake is to turn that local method into a universal claim that every meaningful choice has one discoverable scalar optimum.

## 4. Reasons that can shape a choice

Within the admissible space, reasons can include:

- expected consequences and side effects;
- current value commitments;
- duties, promises, or role obligations;
- uncertainty and robustness;
- reversibility and recoverability;
- preservation of future options;
- timing and urgency;
- resource and opportunity cost;
- coordination with others;
- distribution of benefit, burden, risk, and control;
- learning value;
- identity, project, or long-term direction that the participant reflectively owns.

These reasons may conflict. They do not automatically share one unit.

A responsible decision should make the **material reasons** visible at the strength justified by the context, rather than pretending that every consideration has been reduced to a precise score.

## 5. When reasons underdetermine the choice

Sometimes the available reasons narrow the options but still do not determine one uniquely.

A participant can then use an explicit choice mechanism, for example:

- reflectively committing to one of several supported paths;
- applying a previously accepted priority or convention;
- using a fair allocation or rotation procedure;
- negotiating with affected participants;
- randomizing among genuinely equivalent or procedurally symmetric options;
- deferring when delay itself remains admissible;
- choosing the path that preserves more future maneuverability when that consideration is relevant.

The mechanism should be represented as the actual basis of selection.

`chosen != proven uniquely superior`

`random tie-break != missing qualification repaired`

`preference != authority`

`commitment != permanent truth`

When reasons do not uniquely determine a choice, the system should not fabricate an optimization result merely to make the decision look inevitable.

## 6. Decision creates commitment and path dependence

A decision does more than describe preference. It can close alternatives, allocate resources, create expectations, trigger authorization, or change what is reachable next.

Therefore a decision should be bounded by:

- subject and scope;
- selected option;
- relevant time horizon;
- material reasons and accepted unknowns;
- authority or decision right;
- commitments created;
- expected effects;
- review, reversal, or reopening conditions.

At least preserve:

`considered option != decision`

`decision != authorization`

`authorization != execution`

`execution != real-world effect`

`effect != desired result`

`decision made != decision remains valid indefinitely`

This keeps freedom from being confused with consequence-free action.

## 7. Responsibility under uncertainty

Accountability does not require pretending that all consequences were knowable.

A decision can instead preserve responsibility for:

- the evidence and reasons actually available at the time;
- material unknowns that were knowingly accepted;
- foreseeable effects and affected parties;
- whether a safer or more reversible path was available;
- monitoring the effects that matter;
- correcting, compensating, stopping, or reopening when reality diverges materially;
- not rewriting the historical basis after seeing the outcome.

Therefore:

`bad outcome != decision was necessarily unreasonable when made`

`good outcome != decision process was valid`

`uncertainty acknowledged != responsibility disappears`

Responsibility should remain proportional to actual authority, knowledge, capability, and causal contribution.

## 8. Multi-participant decision

When a choice affects more than one participant, "who decides" becomes part of the decision structure.

At least distinguish:

- who defines the candidate space;
- who supplies evidence;
- who judges qualification;
- who has formal decision authority;
- who is affected;
- whose consent, approval, or representation is required;
- who can appeal, veto, exit, or reopen;
- who bears implementation and recovery responsibility.

Keep:

`individual preference != collective value`

`aggregated preference != legitimate collective decision`

`strategic advantage != decision authority`

`decision authority != legitimacy`

A collective decision may require procedures whose purpose is not to discover a single hidden optimum, but to make power, representation, rights, disagreement, and revision manageable.

## 9. Reopening a decision

A decision is locally binding only while its relevant basis remains sufficient.

Reopening may become warranted when:

- material facts or assumptions change;
- the current boundary is shown to be inadequate;
- capability, authority, dependency, or recovery conditions change;
- a value commitment is revised;
- affected participants or rights were materially omitted;
- observed effects diverge from expected effects;
- irreversible harm or lock-in is developing faster than correction;
- a previously unavailable alternative becomes reachable.

Reopening restores decision space; it does not erase history or automatically select a new option.

`reopen != reverse automatically`

`historical decision invalid now != historical decision never existed`

`new option available != old commitment disappears without transition`

## 10. Common failure modes

**Optimization laundering**: a contestable value choice is hidden inside an objective function and presented as technical necessity.

**Rule laundering**: a specified rule is treated as if satisfying it determines what ought to be chosen.

**Capability determinism**: the easiest or most powerful available action is treated as the preferred action.

**Authority collapse**: ability, information, or expertise is allowed to create decision rights automatically.

**Arbitrariness disguised as freedom**: no reasons or responsibility are preserved because several options were allowed.

**False inevitability**: a choice is presented as uniquely forced even though meaningful alternatives remained.

**Outcome rewriting**: success or failure is used to rewrite what reasons and unknowns existed at decision time.

**Irreversible commitment without need**: options are closed more strongly or earlier than the current purpose requires.

The objective is neither maximum optimization nor maximum spontaneity. It is **bounded, attributable, revisable choice**.

## 11. Relation to the six dimensions and exploration

Free decision uses all six dimensions:

| Dimension | Decision question |
| --- | --- |
| [Distinction](../six-dimensions/distinction.md) | What are the actual candidates, consequences, actors, and boundaries being chosen among? |
| [Qualification](../six-dimensions/qualification.md) | Which transitions, exclusions, commitments, and closure judgments have sufficient basis? |
| [Value](../six-dimensions/value.md) | What directions, commitments, rights, or constraints matter to the choice? |
| [Capability](../six-dimensions/capability.md) | Which candidate paths are currently reachable and recoverable? |
| [Change](../six-dimensions/change.md) | How stable are the premises, and what lock-in or reversibility follows from choosing now? |
| [Others](../six-dimensions/others.md) | Who is affected, who has power or decision rights, and where are alignment and conflict? |

[Exploration](./explore.md) governs whether the current boundary should be reopened. Decision governs choice after the boundary is provisionally accepted.

Neither is above the six dimensions.

## 12. Minimal decision trace

For a materially important decision, it is useful to preserve enough context to reconstruct:

```text
decision context / boundary version
candidate set
material exclusions + basis
remaining admissible options
material reasons and conflicts
accepted unknowns
selected option
decision holder / authority
commitments created
expected effects
review / reversal / reopening conditions
decision time
```

This does not require every decision to become a heavy durable object. The amount of record should be proportional to impact, irreversibility, dispute, delegation, and the need for later reconstruction.

A minimal activity loop is:

```text
provisionally accepted boundary
    ↓
form candidate actions
    ↓
apply independent capability / qualification / authority / hard-constraint boundaries
    ↓
remaining admissible decision space
    ↓
compare material reasons without fabricating a universal scalar
    ↓
choose and create a bounded commitment
    ↓
authorize / execute through the proper downstream boundaries
    ↓
observe reality
    ↓
retain, revise, reverse, or reopen
```

Free decision is therefore not the absence of structure. It is the capacity to **form a real choice where structure does not uniquely decide for the participant, while keeping the reasons, authority, consequences, and possibility of revision visible**.
