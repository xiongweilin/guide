# Exploration Beyond the Current Boundary

[English](./explore.md) | [简体中文](./explore.zh-CN.md)

> Position: an activity mechanism for deciding when and how to reopen the current boundary. It is not a seventh dimension.

Exploration asks: **when is the current distinguishable or represented boundary no longer sufficient for the current purpose, what kinds of missing alternatives may matter, when is further exploration worth its cost, and how should the boundary be reopened?**

The six dimensions describe non-substitutable aspects of the current activity. Exploration begins when the adequacy of that current boundary itself becomes the problem.

## 1. What "outside the boundary" means

The World remains a cognitive boundary. Exploration does not claim that the system can enumerate what lies "outside the World," nor does it turn the unknown into a hidden list waiting to be retrieved.

Here, "outside the current boundary" means content that is not yet adequately represented in the current distinguishable range, distinguished range, candidate space, framing, or model used for the present activity.

At least distinguish:

1. **unknown value inside a known structure**: the object, variable, category, or relation is already represented, but its value or state is unknown;
2. **candidate-space incompleteness**: a relevant variable, object type, boundary, participant, mechanism, scale, acquisition method, validation method, or framing may not yet have been generated;
3. **currently inaccessible content**: something may matter while current sensing, language, tools, interfaces, permissions, resources, or collaborators do not yet provide a usable path to distinguish it.

Therefore:

`unknown value != missing candidate != currently inaccessible`

`not represented != nonexistent != impossible`

`current boundary has gaps != the missing structure is already known`

Exploration is mainly concerned with the second and third cases, while the first may still justify exploration when resolving the value can materially change the decision.

## 2. Signals that the current boundary may be inadequate

A boundary should not be reopened merely because more information could always exist. Reopening becomes relevant when there is evidence that current closure may no longer support the present purpose.

Typical signals include:

- repeated counterexamples or residual cases;
- prediction, explanation, coordination, or intervention failures;
- exceptions accumulating faster than the current structure can absorb them;
- important outcomes changing without corresponding variables in the current model;
- persistent disagreement that cannot be reduced to known factual or value differences;
- failure to transfer a structure across contexts where transfer had been assumed;
- new participants, tools, sensors, interfaces, permissions, or data sources that make previously unavailable distinctions reachable;
- a material change in environment, dependency, timescale, authority, value, or risk;
- high-impact decisions whose outcome is sensitive to an unresolved assumption;
- loss of recovery, exit, or alternative-generation capability.

These are **reopening signals**, not proofs of a specific replacement.

`current structure is under strain != a proposed replacement is correct`

`a surprise occurred != the whole framework must be discarded`

The proportionate response may be to narrow a claim, shorten its validity window, collect one discriminating observation, reopen one local distinction, or reopen the larger framing.

## 3. Whether to explore now

Exploration consumes time, attention, computation, money, coordination capacity, and sometimes real-world risk. The existence of unknowns alone cannot require endless exploration.

Whether to explore now should consider at least:

| Consideration | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **decision sensitivity** | Could a plausible missing distinction materially change the current choice, commitment, authorization, or completion judgment? |
| **discriminating value** | Can an observation or probe meaningfully distinguish important competing framings or candidates? |
| **cost** | What time, attention, computation, money, coordination, or opportunity cost does exploration consume? |
| **risk / irreversibility** | Can the exploration itself cause harm, lock-in, disclosure, rights impact, or irreversible effects? |
| **urgency** | What is lost by delaying the current decision or action? |
| **reversibility of acting now** | Can the current path be tried safely and revised later, or would acting close important future options? |
| **future option value** | Would exploration preserve or create materially useful future choices? |
| **current adequacy** | Is the existing boundary already sufficient for the present purpose even if it is incomplete in principle? |

These considerations do not require one universal scalar objective. Different domains may use thresholds, side constraints, partial ordering, explicit judgment, or specified rules.

Possible dispositions include:

`retain current boundary`

`run a local probe`

`reopen a specific distinction or assumption`

`reopen the larger framing / candidate space`

`defer exploration while preserving the unknown and its trigger`

`stop because exploration is currently unjustified or unsafe`

An exploration transition can itself require [qualification](../six-dimensions/qualification.md). That does not make "exploration qualification" a new basic dimension.

## 4. Generate alternatives without circular admission

A current representation must not require every challenge to be fully translated into its own vocabulary before the challenge is allowed to matter.

Candidate generation can therefore include:

- alternative object boundaries;
- different classifications or ontologies;
- missing participants or stakeholders;
- different timescales;
- alternative causal mechanisms;
- different evidence sources or acquisition interfaces;
- competing problem framings;
- different value framings or constraint sets;
- alternative execution paths or institutional arrangements.

At the challenge stage:

`challenge admission != semantic migration`

A competing framing may expose a blind spot by producing different observations, predictions, failure explanations, or practical results even before full semantic mapping exists.

Mapping becomes necessary when the alternative is to be integrated, federated, migrated into, or made operational inside the current system.

## 5. Explore by discrimination, not accumulation alone

More data is useful only when it changes what can responsibly be distinguished.

A useful probe asks:

> What observation, comparison, experiment, interaction, or limited intervention would most clearly distinguish the important live alternatives?

Possible exploration actions include:

- obtaining an independent source;
- changing the measurement or observation interface;
- asking a participant with a different local world;
- constructing a counterexample;
- testing a boundary case;
- running a reversible experiment;
- varying one relevant condition;
- comparing rival predictions;
- introducing a new tool or representation;
- attempting a bounded alternative path.

The probe should be proportionate to the uncertainty it can resolve.

`more observations != more discriminating evidence`

`model confidence increase != boundary adequacy`

`ability to run an experiment != authority to run it`

Exploratory interventions that affect people, rights, shared resources, privacy, safety, or irreversible state still require independent value, qualification, authority, and capability checks.

## 6. Scope and depth of reopening

Not every tension requires reopening everything.

Exploration can occur at different depths:

1. **value acquisition**: obtain a missing value inside an accepted structure;
2. **local distinction revision**: revise one category, boundary, relationship, or assumption;
3. **candidate-space expansion**: generate alternatives not represented in the current candidate set;
4. **problem reframing**: change the object, predicate, scale, participant set, or completion semantics;
5. **framework challenge**: consider a competing representation that may not share the current framework's vocabulary.

Use the shallowest reopening that can materially address the problem, but do not keep a shallow level merely to protect an inadequate structure.

`local correction sufficient != global reopening required`

`local correction repeatedly fails != continue changing only local values`

## 7. Provisional closure and stopping exploration

Exploration needs a stopping condition or it becomes an infinite obligation.

A current exploration can close provisionally when, for the current purpose:

- remaining live alternatives are unlikely to change the decision materially;
- the important alternatives have been discriminated as far as current means allow;
- additional information has low expected practical value relative to cost;
- delay creates greater cost or risk than the residual uncertainty;
- the next available probe is too risky, unauthorized, or irreversible;
- the activity can proceed through a reversible path that preserves later correction;
- the unresolved part can be explicitly carried forward as unknown.

Closure should preserve:

- what was explored;
- what alternatives were rejected and on what basis;
- what remains unknown;
- what boundary is being accepted provisionally;
- what would trigger review or reopening;
- what new tools, permissions, evidence, or events would make further exploration worthwhile.

Therefore:

`stop exploring != nothing remains unknown`

`provisional closure != permanent completeness`

`cannot explore safely now != the missing candidate does not exist`

## 8. Common failure modes

**Premature closure**: current categories are treated as complete because they are convenient or already implemented.

**Endless exploration**: the existence of possible unknowns is used to avoid any finite commitment.

**Schema lock-in**: only candidates expressible in the current ontology are admitted.

**Novelty bias**: a new framing is preferred merely because it is new.

**Accumulation without discrimination**: more data is collected without improving the distinction among important alternatives.

**Unsafe probing**: information value is allowed to substitute for authority, rights, safety, or reversibility.

**False impossibility**: no path or candidate has been found, so the system reports that none can exist.

**Global reopening from local failure**: a bounded mismatch is used to discard structures that still work for the current purpose.

The objective is not maximum openness. It is **proportionate reopenability**.

## 9. Relation to the six dimensions

Exploration uses all six dimensions without replacing them:

| Dimension | Exploration question |
| --- | --- |
| [Distinction](../six-dimensions/distinction.md) | What current boundary, default distinction, or candidate space may need reopening? |
| [Qualification](../six-dimensions/qualification.md) | Is there sufficient basis to explore, to stop exploring, or to adopt a revised boundary? |
| [Value](../six-dimensions/value.md) | Which unknowns matter enough to spend resources or accept exploration risk? |
| [Capability](../six-dimensions/capability.md) | What observations, tools, collaborators, or interventions are actually reachable? |
| [Change](../six-dimensions/change.md) | Is the boundary failing because the structure is transitioning, unstable, or stale? |
| [Others](../six-dimensions/others.md) | Who has different information, framings, incentives, authority, or exposure to the exploration? |

Exploration is therefore not a master dimension. It is an activity that coordinates the six dimensions when the adequacy of the current boundary is itself in question.

## 10. Handoff to decision and reality feedback

A minimal loop is:

```text
current boundary
    ↓
tension / opportunity / material uncertainty
    ↓
qualify whether reopening is warranted
    ↓
generate competing candidates
    ↓
obtain discriminating evidence through bounded probes
    ↓
revise / narrow / retain the boundary
    ↓
provisional closure
    ↓
decision within the accepted boundary
    ↓
action and reality-side feedback
    ↓
retain, recalibrate, or reopen again
```

If the current boundary is already sufficient for the current purpose, exploration can legitimately stop before generating new candidates.

If reality-side feedback later breaks the assumptions that supported closure, the boundary can be reopened again.

The point is not to discover a final complete World. The point is to know **when the current boundary is enough to proceed, when it is not, and how to change it without pretending that the unknown was already known**.
