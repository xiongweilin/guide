# Distinction

[English](./distinction.md) | [简体中文](./distinction.zh-CN.md)

Distinction asks: **what can enter the current world, and what has not yet entered it?**

It is not a final ontology of the World. It is the foundational dimension through which finite humans and their extended systems form usable structures of reality.

## Basic structure

`World (cognitive boundary) → distinguishable world / distinguishable range → acquisition → distinguished range → current representation`

- **World (cognitive boundary)**: current observation, language, classifications, and models are not entitled by default to exhaust the World itself.
- **Distinguishable world**: aspects of reality that can enter distinction, comparison, relation, and verification under some sensing, language, tool, interaction, and capability conditions.
- **Distinguishable range**: distinctions that could in principle be formed under current conditions, including candidates not yet actually formed.
- **Distinguished range**: distinctions already acquired, formed, and currently invocable; it may be incomplete or mistaken.
- **Current representation**: objects, concepts, classifications, relations, structures, and judgments further organized from what has been distinguished.

Therefore:

`absent from current representation != nonexistent != impossible`

## Default distinctions

Human activity does not start from a structureless point. Language, culture, education, interfaces, institutions, historical decisions, existing models, and personal experience preload many classifications and boundaries.

These are **default distinctions**.

They reduce cognitive cost, but they do not prove that:

- the boundary comes from the World itself;
- the classification is valid for the current problem;
- competing distinctions do not exist;
- the current distinction should remain permanently fixed.

Many persistent errors are not wrong answers but default distinctions that were never reopened as distinctions.

## Questions arise from distinctions

A sentence being askable only establishes a question candidate.

Before answering, inspect:

- whether object boundaries are clear;
- whether the predicate applies to that object;
- whether multiple subjects, scales, or processes were compressed into one object;
- which default distinctions and presuppositions were silently introduced;
- whether the current distinguished range was mistaken for the whole distinguishable range;
- whether the question survives under an alternative distinction.

For example, “What is the meaning of life?” presupposes that “life” can be treated as one object, that “meaning” can be its global property, and that the answer should exist in a discoverable form. The question may still be useful, but those presuppositions should themselves become inspectable.

## Acquisition errors and distinction errors

Keep separate:

- a signal was not acquired;
- the signal was acquired but not correctly distinguished;
- a distinction was formed but the classification boundary was wrong;
- relations were organized incorrectly;
- a structure holds only locally;
- important variables, participants, scales, or candidates have not yet appeared.

Cognitive revision therefore includes both:

`updating values inside existing distinctions`

and

`reopening distinctions, classifications, boundaries, and candidate spaces`

The second cannot be flattened into the first.

## Distinction among multiple people

Different people can inhabit the same World while having different distinguishable ranges, distinguished ranges, languages, and default classifications.

So “we are looking at the same thing” can still hide differences in:

- acquisition;
- distinctions;
- question structure;
- callable evidence.

Coordination sometimes requires exchanging facts and sometimes rebuilding a shared distinction structure first.

## Boundary with the other dimensions

A candidate being distinguished does not mean it has [qualification](./qualification.md). Distinguishing a norm does not mean it is one's [value](./value.md). Failure to distinguish a path does not prove lack of [capability](./capability.md). A usable current classification does not prove long-term [stability](./change.md). One person's distinction scheme cannot silently become the structure that [others](./others.md) must accept.
