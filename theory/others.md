# Others

[English](./others.md) | [简体中文](./others.zh-CN.md)

Others asks: **who affects whom, and in which dimensions are they aligned or in conflict?**

Human activity is not an isolated internal process. Individuals, families, teams, organizations, institutions, and technical systems can change one another's information, costs, values, capabilities, and reachable paths.

## Minimal coordinates

`relation strength × alignment / conflict`

### Relation strength

Relation strength asks:

> How much does one participant's state, choice, or action change another participant's reality, information, cost, value, capability, or reachable path?

Strong relation does not imply alignment. Weak relation does not imply no relation.

### Alignment / conflict

Alignment and conflict must be located in a dimension rather than applied as one total label to the whole relationship.

Participants may have:

- aligned factual judgments but conflicting values;
- aligned values but conflicting methods;
- aligned goals but competition for resources;
- short-term alignment and long-term conflict;
- different distinction schemes while still having compatible actions.

Social relations therefore cannot be reduced to a simple cooperation/opposition binary.

## Local worlds

Different participants do not automatically share the same distinguished world.

They may differ in:

- acquired information;
- distinctions;
- question structure;
- callable evidence;
- time scale;
- values and goals.

Coordination sometimes requires exchanging information, sometimes rebuilding shared distinctions, and sometimes accepting value conflicts that cannot be eliminated.

## Strategic interaction

Strategic interaction exists whenever one party's judgment or action changes another party's future choices.

In such environments:

- behavior is not the same as observation;
- observation is not the same as correct interpretation;
- correct interpretation is not the same as sufficient evidence;
- models, predictions, and rules can themselves change the behavior being modeled.

Published forecasts, metrics, incentives, and institutional rules can therefore alter the subsequent data-generating process.

## Authority, recognition, and legitimacy

Multi-participant activity should keep separate:

- **formal authority**: who has decision rights under the institution;
- **factual recognition**: whether others actually recognize that identity or authority;
- **legitimacy**: whether the authority or rule has sufficient normative basis.

`strategic advantage != formal authority != recognition != legitimacy`

They can interact, but they cannot substitute for one another.

## Institutions

Institutions do not need to eliminate difference. Their role is to provide inspectable mechanisms for high-relation, potentially conflicting activity:

- problem framing;
- decision procedure;
- authority boundaries;
- commitment;
- review;
- exit;
- correction and reopening.

A durable institution supports stable cooperation while retaining the ability to renegotiate when facts, values, and relationship structures change.

## Boundary with the other dimensions

Alignment and conflict among multiple people can occur in [distinction](./distinction.md), [qualification](./qualification.md), [value](./value.md), [capability](./capability.md), or [change](./change.md). The others dimension describes how those differences become coupled through relationships rather than replacing the first five dimensions.
