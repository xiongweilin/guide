# Change

[English](./change.md) | [简体中文](./change.zh-CN.md)

Change asks: **are we facing a persistent structure, a transition process, or a situation with no stable structure yet?**

Time itself is not the central issue. The issue is how structure persists, fails, and reorganizes at a relevant scale.

## Basic structure

`stable / transitional / no stable structure`

### Stable

Within a given time scale, scope, and disturbance range, a relation or organization persists enough to serve as a working object.

Stable does not mean eternal or motionless. It means that a usable invariant exists at the current scale.

### Transitional

The current form is mainly a process of moving from one organization toward another.

More important than static classification are:

- direction;
- rate;
- transition conditions;
- path;
- irreversibility;
- which old structures are failing;
- which new structures are forming.

Being transitional does not mean the destination is already known.

### No stable structure

At the current scale there is not enough persistence to support a reliable fixed model.

Forcing stable objects, classifications, or rules in this condition can manufacture a false entity. A better response may be to preserve unknowns, shorten judgment validity, increase observation, or delay closure.

## Multiple time scales

The same activity can contain:

- fast variables: one request, local action, short-term fluctuation;
- medium variables: workflows, habits, team practice, policy;
- slow variables: identity, core interfaces, institutional boundaries, long-term relationships.

A structure can be stable in the short term and unstable in the long term. A long-lived structure can contain many rapid local changes.

“What changed?” therefore requires a declared scale.

## Closure, revalidation, and reopening

Finite systems need provisional closure:

`open → converge → provisional closure → persist → conditions change → review / reopen`

Historical validity does not imply current validity.

Material changes in evidence, scope, dependencies, environment, authority, value, identity, cost, or risk should trigger revalidation.

Reopening does not generate a new answer automatically. It restores candidate and choice space.

## Path dependence and lock-in

Change depends not only on the current state but also on the path used to reach it.

Some actions:

- consume future options;
- increase switching cost;
- create dependency;
- harden classifications and institutions;
- turn temporary arrangements into default permanent structure.

So both current state and future maneuverability matter.

A system can look stable while losing its ability to exit, substitute, recover, or distinguish differently. That is a form of lock-in.

## Boundary with the other dimensions

[Distinction](./distinction.md) determines what is currently treated as structure. [Qualification](./qualification.md) determines when it can remain or must reopen. [Value](./value.md) can be revised through experience. [Capability](./capability.md) determines whether paths can be changed. [Others](./others.md) couples multiple change processes together.
