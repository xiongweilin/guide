# Capability

[English](./capability.md) | [简体中文](./capability.zh-CN.md)

Capability asks: **which outcomes are currently realizable, and through what causal structures?**

Capability is not the sum of skills stored inside a person. It is the set of reachable paths that can actually be invoked under current conditions.

## Basic expansion structure

`cannot → self-capable → assisted-capable → leverage-capable`

- **Cannot**: no feasible path is currently available, or necessary conditions are missing.
- **Self-capable**: achievable through one's body, knowledge, skills, and directly controlled resources.
- **Assisted-capable**: achievable through tools, devices, AI, professional services, institutional interfaces, or other external capability.
- **Leverage-capable**: achievable without personally carrying the main causal process, by using environment, rules, networks, incentives, cooperation, automation, or existing trends.

The core question becomes:

> Which causal structures can the current participant invoke?

Much of modern human capability is distributed across infrastructure, tools, organizations, institutions, and relationships rather than located inside one body or mind.

## Reachability is conditional

Whether a target is reachable depends on:

- current resources;
- time window;
- tools and interfaces;
- collaborators;
- permissions;
- dependencies;
- environmental state;
- affordable cost;
- available recovery and exit paths.

“I can do this” therefore always has a scope and set of conditions.

## Capability, permission, and value

`can do != authorized to do != worth doing`

Technical capability does not create authority. Authority does not create technical capability. Value endorsement creates neither automatically.

For actions with real-world effects, also keep:

`plan can be formed → technically executable → authorized → executed → effect realized → outcome verified`

These states cannot be flattened into one notion of success.

## Dependencies and leverage

Assisted and leverage capability introduce dependency networks.

At minimum distinguish:

1. normal-operation dependencies;
2. failure-propagation dependencies;
3. recovery dependencies.

Redundancy on the normal path does not prove redundancy on the recovery path.

The more capability relies on leverage, the less direct execution may be required from the person, but dependence on rules, networks, infrastructure, and other people often increases. Capability expansion can therefore expand exposure and lock-in at the same time.

## Maneuverability

Strong capability includes not only executing the current plan but preserving the ability to change direction later:

- generate alternatives;
- switch tools or dependencies;
- stop effects;
- recover control;
- absorb transition cost;
- rebuild prerequisites and authority;
- reconstruct what happened.

The deepest capability loss is not merely failing to do one thing. It is losing the ability to generate new paths and regain control.

## Boundary with the other dimensions

Capability depends on paths made visible by [distinction](./distinction.md), needs [qualification](./qualification.md) before entering governed action, receives direction from [value](./value.md), changes as environments and structures [change](./change.md), and is often acquired, amplified, or constrained through [others](./others.md) and institutions.
