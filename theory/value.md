# Value

[English](./value.md) | [简体中文](./value.zh-CN.md)

> Inspiration: the tension between what one wants to do and what one ought to do.

Value asks: **what is worth pursuing, avoiding, maintaining, committing to, or refusing?**

Facts describe what is. Value gives direction. Knowing how the world is does not automatically determine where activity should go.

## Basic tension

`external norms ↔ internal norms`

- **External norms**: requirements or expectations from family, culture, education, law, institutions, roles, organizations, markets, groups, and relationships.
- **Internal norms**: directions arising from experience, preference, identity, reflection, active choice, and commitment.

This is not a simple binary. External norms can be internalized until their source is no longer noticed; internal norms can change through experience and reflection.

Keep separate:

- where a norm came from;
- whether the person recognizes its source;
- whether it is merely habit or pressure;
- whether it has reflective endorsement;
- whether it has become a durable commitment;
- whether it can bind others;
- whether public legitimacy is required.

“I feel this is my value” does not by itself prove independence from external norms.

## Four common layers of value

### Functional

A system approaches, avoids, maintains, or regulates certain outcomes.

This can appear as habit, reward, control objective, or physiological regulation without automatically constituting normative value.

### Experiential

A state may have positive or negative experienced valence, if subjective experience is present.

Functional performance does not by itself prove subjective experience.

### Reflective

A person can inspect preferences, identity, and commitments and form reflective endorsement or rejection.

Reflective endorsement makes “why I live this way” revisable rather than merely habitual.

### Public normative

When choices affect others, rights, shared resources, or institutions, personal value alone cannot determine public rules. Authority, procedure, and legitimacy become additional requirements.

Higher layers cannot be automatically inferred from lower ones.

## Source and validity

Values can arise from the body, relationships, education, institutions, habits, path dependence, loss, trauma, imitation, reflective endorsement, or rejection.

But:

`explaining a source != proving invalidity`

`personal endorsement != authority over others`

`social acceptance != automatic legitimacy`

Source, endorsement, validity, and public binding force are separate questions.

## Commitment and reopening

Commitment does not permanently freeze value.

Coercion, manipulation, lack of exit, severe dependency, changed factual basis, or persistent incompatibility can weaken the basis for treating an old commitment as still valid.

Reopening a value judgment does not reveal one hidden “true value.” It means only that the old qualification can no longer be assumed to continue.

## Boundary with the other dimensions

The objects and norm sources involved in value must first be [distinguished](./distinction.md). A value entering commitment, rule, or action needs independent [qualification](./qualification.md). “Worth doing” does not imply current [capability](./capability.md). Values can change with experience and structure [change](./change.md). When values affect multiple people, [others](./others.md) introduces alignment, conflict, authority, and legitimacy.
