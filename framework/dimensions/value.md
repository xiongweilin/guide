# Value

[English](./value.md) | [简体中文](./value.zh-CN.md)

Value asks: **what is worth pursuing, avoiding, maintaining, committing to, or refusing?**

Facts describe what is. Value provides direction. Knowing how reality is does not automatically determine where it should be moved.

## 1. External and internal norms

`external norms ↔ internal norms`

- **External norms**: requirements or expectations from family, culture, education, law, institutions, roles, organizations, markets, groups, and relationships.
- **Internal norms**: directions formed through experience, preference, identity, reflection, active choice, and commitment.

External norms can become deeply internalized; internal norms can change through experience and reflection.

At minimum distinguish:

- source;
- whether the subject recognizes the source;
- whether it is habit, pressure, or regulation rather than reflection;
- whether it has been reflectively endorsed;
- whether it forms durable commitment;
- whether it has standing to constrain others;
- whether public justification is required.

`I experience this as my value != it is independent of external influence`

## 2. Four analytical layers

At minimum preserve:

`descriptive / functional != experiential != reflective commitment != public normative`

These are not required developmental stages.

### Descriptive / functional

Outcome difference only means states differ. System relevance means a difference changes state, resources, relations, or reachable paths. Regulatory polarity means the system forms approach, avoidance, maintenance, recovery, or search directions.

`regulatory polarity != subjective experience != normative conclusion`

### Experiential

Before treating an object as having positive or negative experience, there should be proportionate grounds for experiential standing.

Self-maintenance, reward/loss, goal representation, complex behavior, self-models, verbal reports, learning, and adaptation can all be evidence, but none alone automatically proves experiential subjecthood.

Experiential standing may remain `unknown` or `contested`. Severe irreversible welfare risk may enter decision under uncertainty without being reported as proof of subjectivity.

### Reflective commitment

First-order orientations can arise from body, experience, habit, teaching, relationships, institutions, strategic interaction, or external tasks.

Reflective endorsement makes these orientations, sources, consequences, and conflicts objects of judgment and accepts, revises, suspends, or rejects them.

An individual value commitment can organize later action while remaining revisable under reality-side cost.

`revisable != no commitment`

`commitment formed != permanently exempt from revision`

### Public normative

Individual value does not automatically create authority over others.

When action affects others, rights, shared resources, third parties, or institutions, additional structures are needed: public norms, procedures, authority, representation, appeal, and legitimacy.

`normatively relevant fact != normative conclusion`

## 3. Normative standing and rights

At minimum preserve:

`has preference != has interest != is affected != has moral standing != has procedural standing != has decision power != has veto != has authority`

A material rights claim should identify where possible:

- rights holder;
- protected interest or freedom;
- party constrained or obligated;
- grounds and scope;
- remedy, recognition, or enforcement.

`source of a right != justification != recognition != enforcement`

guide does not choose one final moral theory. It requires material normative conflicts to remain visible.

## 4. Value conflict and public decision

Value conflict need not collapse into one numerical scale. It may involve:

- comparable trade-offs;
- thresholds or side constraints;
- priority conflicts;
- partial comparability or incomparability;
- tragic conflict where every available path violates important values.

The framework permits:

`value A sufficiently grounded + value B sufficiently grounded + material conflict + no sufficiently grounded common scale`

In multi-participant activity also preserve:

`individual preference != aggregate preference != collective value != legitimate decision`

Voting, summation, ranking, market price, or welfare measures can be useful tools, but cannot silently become complete public justification.

`normative truth claim != consent != public defensibility != procedural legitimacy != authority`

## 5. Source and validity

Values can arise from body, experience, care, relationships, education, institutions, incentives, habit, path dependence, loss, trauma, imitation, strategic interaction, reflective endorsement, or rejection.

`explaining source != proving invalidity`

`natural origin != normative legitimacy`

`social origin != false value`

`individual endorsement != authority over others`

`widely accepted != automatically legitimate`

## 6. Manipulation, coercion, and real exit

When evaluating external influence, ask not only whether value has social origins, but whether the influence structure contains:

- systematic deception or information control;
- threats, punishment, or resource control;
- blocked alternative information, relationships, or exit;
- exploitation of dependency, vulnerability, or power asymmetry;
- continued reflective endorsement after pressure is removed.

Finding manipulation or coercion weakens the sufficiency of treating a surface commitment as free and stable. It does not automatically reveal what the subject "really should want."

Nominal exit is not real exit. Real exit depends on resources, safety, identity, relationships, knowledge, time, and institutional alternatives.

## 7. Commitment and reopening

Value commitments are not permanently frozen.

Reopening can be supported when:

- experience, first-order orientation, and reflective commitment remain in long conflict;
- multiple commitments cannot jointly persist;
- key factual grounds are refuted;
- pursuing the value repeatedly creates major external loss;
- body, relationships, environment, or identity materially change;
- coercion, manipulation, severe dependency, or lack of real exit is discovered.

Reopening does not imply a hidden "true value" waiting to be found. It means the old grounds are no longer sufficient to continue automatically.

## 8. Relation to the other dimensions

Value depends on distinguished objects, relations, consequences, and participants but cannot be replaced by facts, capability, causal influence, or majority preference.

`can do != worth doing`

`high probability != should pursue`

`strategic advantage != legitimacy`

`reflective commitment != authority over others`

Moving value into action still requires sufficiency, decision, and the action chain.
