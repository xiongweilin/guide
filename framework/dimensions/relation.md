# Relation

[English](./relation.md) | [简体中文](./relation.zh-CN.md)

Relation asks: **how are already distinguished contents connected, composed, dependent, constrained, and mutually affected?**

Relation is not limited to relations among people. Objects, variables, organizations, roles, resources, interfaces, rules, network nodes, identities, and institutions can all form relational structures.

## 1. Relation is not causality

Relation can represent:

- composition / membership;
- dependency;
- spatial or network position;
- identity and role;
- ownership / use / delegation;
- information visibility;
- authority and obligation;
- similarity / conflict / alignment;
- resources and exchange;
- participation and representation;
- relation strength.

But:

`relation exists != causal effect exists`

`correlation != causation`

Causality needs additional intervention, mechanism, comparison, or counterfactual grounds.

## 2. Relation strength and alignment / conflict

For people and organizations, one useful minimal representation is:

`relation strength × alignment / conflict`

Relation strength asks:

> To what extent does one party's state, choice, or action change another party's information, costs, resources, possibilities, values, or reality-side conditions?

Alignment and conflict must identify the dimension in which they occur instead of labeling the whole relationship.

Participants may agree on facts but conflict on values, agree on values but conflict on methods, share goals while competing for resources, or align short-term while conflicting long-term.

`strong relation != alignment`

`weak relation != no relation`

## 3. Local worlds and models of others

Different finite systems have different current boundaries:

`B_i != B_j`

A system may form a working representation of another actor's capability, resources, values, authority, information, action candidates, and judgment style, but:

`my model of another != the other's real internal state`

Higher-order modeling such as "I think you think..." is worth adding only when another level can materially change candidates, risk, or discriminating information. Infinite recursion is not required for useful finite modeling.

## 4. Power topology

Relation strength cannot substitute for power type.

At minimum preserve:

- **definition power**: shaping problem boundaries, candidate spaces, standards, or agendas;
- **judgment power**: deciding whether grounds are sufficient;
- **decision power**: turning judgments into commitments, resource allocation, or binding choices.

Execution, verification, resources, information, exit, stopping, review, and reopening can be represented separately when they materially change the analysis.

`formal role != actual control`

`decision power != power to define candidate set`

`power under current rules != power to change the rules`

Concentration matters especially when one actor or one failure domain controls several positions that are intended to check one another.

## 5. Collective action, organizations, and networks

Aligned interests do not automatically produce collective action:

`aligned interests != coordination completed != durable collective capability`

When internal coalitions, incentives, authority, or information differences change outcomes, an organization cannot be modeled as a scaled-up individual.

`formal organizational goal != actual internal incentives`

`formal power != actual internal control`

`many capable individuals != capable collective`

Pairwise relations do not exhaust network structure. Network position changes access to information, opportunity, bargaining power, dependency, diffusion, exposure, exit, and reachability.

Identity also has different semantic positions:

`self-identity != others' classification != institutionally recognized status`

These positions can affect value, authority, and possibility simultaneously.

## 6. Action, signal, and evidence

`action != signal != evidence conclusion`

The same offer, silence, delay, refusal, cooperation, or statement may not be acquired, may be distinguished differently by different observers, or may support competing interpretations.

Silence must not default to consent, refusal, ignorance, or strategy.

When interpretation fails, separate problems in reality-side input, acquisition, distinction, relation modeling, evidence strength, causal explanation, or the broader strategic representation.

## 7. Strategy and reflexivity

Strategic interaction does not presuppose a fully specified "true game." A finite system may not know:

- which actors matter;
- which actions are feasible;
- what information is visible to whom;
- how another actor distinguishes and judges;
- which time scale matters;
- whether the interaction is framed correctly.

Game models, optimizers, and simulators are downstream tools. A solver requiring complete fields is not a reason to fill reality-side unknowns with false precision.

Relational systems can also be reflexive:

`model → judgment → action → reality changes → others adapt → relations change again`

Public predictions, metrics, incentives, institutional rules, and exposed strategies can change the modeled actors.

## 8. Authority, recognition, and legitimacy

Multi-participant activity should separate:

- **formal authority**: who has decision power under the current institution and scope;
- **recognition**: who treats an identity, commitment, or authority as a basis for coordination in a given scope;
- **legitimacy**: whether that authority, rule, or recognition has sufficient normative grounds.

`strategic advantage != formal authority != recognition != legitimacy`

Recognition is a relational fact, not a global boolean. Widespread recognition also does not automatically establish normative legitimacy.

## 9. Institutions and meta-power

Institutions can provide problem framing, procedures, authority, representation, commitments, handoffs, review, appeal, exit, renegotiation, and reopening.

But institutions can also reproduce themselves:

`institution → incentives / constraints → actor adaptation → resource and capability distribution → future bargaining power → institutional reproduction`

`institution persists != institution remains normatively justified`

`nominal exit exists != real exit path exists`

Also preserve:

`decision power != agenda-setting power`

`choosing among given options != defining the option set`

When such structures change participants' possibility spaces, relation must be able to represent them without requiring one universal political or organizational theory.
