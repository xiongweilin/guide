# Causality

[English](./causality.md) | [简体中文](./causality.zh-CN.md)

> Position: this dimension is a **working lens** for describing a purposeful finite actor's current boundary, not an irreducible ontological primitive. The six lenses prevent material semantic substitution; see [`../foundations/minimal-derivation.md`](../foundations/minimal-derivation.md) for the minimal derivation.

Causality asks: **what changes cause what other changes, and what interventions can change which outcomes?**

It separates "appears together or is related" from "changing one condition changes another outcome" and supplies effect hypotheses for the action chain.

## 1. Observation, intervention, and counterfactual

At minimum preserve:

`what was observed != what happens under intervention != what would have happened otherwise`

`correlation != causal effect`

`more data != causal identifiability`

A consequential causal claim should make explicit where possible:

- cause or candidate intervention;
- outcome;
- target and scope;
- context and boundary conditions;
- time scale;
- relevant comparison;
- key assumptions and evidence;
- competing mechanisms.

guide does not require one causal method. Experiments, natural experiments, structural models, mechanism evidence, domain knowledge, statistical identification, and other methods may be used when claim strength matches grounds.

## 2. Identifiability and structural assumptions

Causal identifiability depends on structural assumptions rather than sample size alone.

When material, consider:

- confounding;
- selection bias;
- measurement error;
- mediators and colliders;
- interference among units;
- feedback;
- environmental transfer;
- participant adaptation.

`enough data != structural assumptions automatically valid`

When key assumptions remain insufficient, causal status should remain unknown, competing, or locally valid rather than being forced closed.

## 3. Causality and actionable capability

Even an established causal effect does not imply that the current purposeful finite actor can call that path.

`causal relation != intervention interface != callable path != authorization`

For a causal path to become current callable capability, it additionally needs:

- accessible intervention interface;
- required resources;
- correct timing;
- valid dependencies;
- allowed scope;
- acceptable risk;
- necessary authority;
- recovery or exit path.

This is why capability is not a separate dimension here: it is a callable structure jointly formed by causality, possibility, relation, temporality, and purposeful-actor interfaces.

## 4. Dependencies, failure, and recovery

An action path may have at least three types of dependency:

1. normal-operation dependency;
2. failure-propagation dependency;
3. recovery dependency.

Redundancy on the normal path does not imply redundancy on the recovery path.

Tools, organizations, institutions, infrastructure, and others can amplify capability while also introducing shared failure domains, lock-in, and failure propagation.

`role separation != failure independence`

Multiple models, verifiers, or reviewers that share the same data, model, identity infrastructure, authority source, technical root, or incentive structure may fail for the same reason.

## 5. Cross-environment transfer

A causal effect established in one environment does not automatically transfer to another.

`causal effect there != same causal effect here`

Cross-domain or cross-context reuse should distinguish:

1. **common problem**: only the question or framing transfers;
2. **formal similarity**: representation or computation may transfer;
3. **mechanism similarity**: prediction or intervention experience transfers only when objects, boundary conditions, and mechanisms are sufficiently similar.

`formal similarity != mechanism evidence`

## 6. Multi-participant and reflexive causality

In social, organizational, and network systems, intervention may first change other actors, who then change the outcome.

Public models, metrics, rules, and strategies can also change the modeled object:

`model → action → others adapt → new reality → causal structure changes`

A relationship that later fails may mean the original model was wrong, or that action changed the original causal conditions.

## 7. Causal chain and effect attribution

A target state occurring after action does not prove that the current action caused it. Concurrent actions, manual operations, other actors, or external changes may have produced the same endpoint.

If the system claims "this action produced this result," it should preserve enough of the chain:

`decision → authorization → execution → external effect → observation → verification → outcome`

Reality-side verification and effect provenance cannot be replaced by the executor's own success response.

## 8. Causality and value

Causality describes what changes what. It does not answer whether the change should be made.

`can cause an outcome != outcome worth causing`

`high causal influence != decision authority`

`accurate prediction != permission to act`

Interventions affecting people, rights, privacy, safety, or irreversible state still require independent value, relation, possibility, temporality, and sufficiency judgments.

## 9. Causal prediction versus a successful forecast

When reasoning about the future, distinguish conditional predictions within formal models, empirical forecasts under a data-generating distribution, and predictions about the effect of interventions. An observed forecast hit supports neither causal attribution nor an untested claim about another setting. Suitable comparators or identification grounds remain necessary.

For material transitions, preserve ex ante targets, horizons, model/data versions, applicability conditions, and proportionate uncertainty; compare against appropriate independent observations when outcomes become available. Keep unavailable outcomes unknown. When discrepancies arise, check identification assumptions, transport, strategic adaptation, and measurement change. See the [judgment and prediction validation protocol](../judgment-validation.md).
