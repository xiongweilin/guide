# Temporality

[English](./temporality.md) | [简体中文](./temporality.zh-CN.md)

> Position: this dimension is a **working lens** for describing a purposeful finite actor's current boundary, not an irreducible ontological primitive. The six lenses prevent material semantic substitution; see [`../../minimal-derivation.md`](../../minimal-derivation.md) for the minimal derivation.

Temporality asks: **what happens before or after what, for how long and at what rate, and when does a structure remain valid, transition, or fail?**

Temporality is more than timestamps. It covers ordering, duration, rhythm, deadlines, stability, transition, hysteresis, path dependence, and relations among time scales.

## 1. Order and duration

The same objects and relations can produce different reality-side outcomes when their order differs.

At minimum distinguish:

- before / after;
- simultaneity and overlap;
- duration;
- time windows;
- deadlines;
- cycles and frequency;
- delay;
- validity period;
- historical lineage.

`same state != same path`

`same current value != same route of arrival`

A valid endpoint cannot retroactively prove a valid historical transition.

## 2. Stable, transitional, and no stable structure

Under a given scope, time scale, and disturbance range:

- **stable**: structure persists enough to serve provisionally as a working object;
- **transitional**: the current form is mainly a process of moving from one organization to another;
- **no stable structure**: no organization persists enough at the current scale for reliable fixed modeling.

Stable does not mean eternal. Transitional does not mean the destination is known. No stable structure does not mean "nothing exists"; it means the system should not force a stable object where current evidence does not support one.

A proportionate response may be to retain unknown, shorten validity, increase observation frequency, or delay closure.

## 3. Multiple time scales

The same activity may contain:

- fast variables: one request, local action, short-term fluctuation;
- medium variables: workflows, habits, team practices, policy;
- slow variables: identity, core interfaces, institutional boundaries, long relationships.

`short-term stable != long-term stable`

`long-term stable structure != no rapid local change`

Any claim that "something changed" should specify the relevant scale.

## 4. Rate compatibility

A correction mechanism can exist formally yet be too slow to matter.

Compare:

`detect + judge + stop + recover + reauthorize`

with:

`error propagation + damage accumulation + lock-in`

If correction takes as long as or longer than the window in which major irreversible effects form, the system may be structurally unable to correct in time.

`correction mechanism exists != correction speed sufficient`

Rate compatibility is not a universal preference for faster or slower behavior; it compares the dynamics of the processes involved.

## 5. Hysteresis and effective reversibility

The path into a state need not be the reverse of the path out:

`entry path != exit path`

Dependencies, trust, concentration, information propagation, institutional change, and accumulated damage can create hysteresis.

Effective reversibility distinguishes:

- **state reversibility**: can the reality-side state return to acceptable conditions?
- **control reversibility**: can the system regain the ability to stop, modify, take over, migrate, or exit?
- **epistemic reversibility**: do evidence, knowledge, people, records, dissent channels, and verification capability remain available?

A formal rollback may restore only one layer.

## 6. Path dependence and lock-in

Current possibility is shaped not only by current state but also by the route taken.

Some actions:

- consume future options;
- raise switching costs;
- harden classifications or institutions;
- create dependencies;
- turn temporary arrangements into defaults;
- reduce future ability to distinguish again or recover control.

A system may look stable while already locked in if it is losing exit, substitution, and recovery capacity.

## 7. Current validity

`historically valid != currently valid`

Material changes in facts, scope, dependencies, authority, value, cost, risk, relations, or environment can require revalidation.

Freshness should not mean only "recent timestamp." It should mean that the dependencies supporting the current claim remain valid.

Reopening restores candidate and choice space; it does not automatically provide a new answer.
