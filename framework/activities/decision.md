# Decision

[English](./decision.md) | [简体中文](./decision.zh-CN.md)

Decision is the activity of choosing when the current boundary is **provisionally sufficient** for the current purpose. It is not a dimension and does not assert a metaphysical theory of free will.

Decision asks:

> When current facts, rules, constraints, possibilities, and values do not uniquely force one action, how can a purposeful finite actor form a choice that has reasons, is attributable, remains revisable, and carries proportionate responsibility for consequences?

## 1. Preconditions: boundary provisionally sufficient

Before a consequential decision, check at least:

- problem framing is sufficient;
- important facts and unknowns are represented with proportionate strength;
- no missing candidate is reasonably likely to change the choice;
- relations, power, and authority boundaries are clear enough;
- causal paths have sufficient support;
- temporal validity matches reality-side change speed;
- reachability, controllability, and recovery paths are clear enough;
- hard constraints, obligations, rights, and value conflicts are represented.

If missing content could materially change the decision and has a reasonable acquisition path, return to [Exploration](./exploration.md).

`incomplete in principle != unable to decide`

`material boundary deficiency != pretend completeness and continue`

## 2. Current admissible decision space

Let (C) be the currently distinguished candidate set.

Candidates may be excluded for different reasons:

- not a coherent action under the current framing;
- causal path lacks sufficient support;
- currently known unreachable;
- required dependency or recovery condition absent;
- sufficiency gate for the transition not met;
- outside current authority;
- violates applicable hard constraints, rights, contracts, safety boundaries, or explicit commitments.

The remainder (F) is the current admissible decision space.

Exclusion grounds must remain distinct:

`unreachable != unauthorized`

`unauthorized != valueless`

`value conflict != causal failure`

`currently excluded != permanently wrong`

If (F) is empty, do not fabricate a choice. Change the goal, obtain capability or authority, renegotiate constraints, explore again, or stop.

## 3. No requirement for one optimum

Several options can remain reasonable:

- some consequences comparable and others not;
- tied under current standards;
- only partially ordered;
- supported by different sufficiently grounded values;
- differently sensitive to future conditions;
- all within authority and hard constraints.

`multiple admissible options != hidden unique correct option must exist`

`partial order != unable to decide`

`no common numerical scale != no reasons`

Optimization is appropriate when a domain's objectives, measurements, constraints, and aggregation rules are sufficiently established. It should not be generalized into a claim that every important choice has one numerical optimum.

## 4. Decision reasons

Reasons may include:

- expected consequences and side effects;
- current value commitments;
- obligations, roles, and prior commitments;
- uncertainty and robustness;
- reversibility and recovery;
- preservation of future options;
- timing and urgency;
- resources and opportunity cost;
- coordination with others;
- distribution of benefits, burdens, risk, and control;
- learning value;
- reflectively endorsed identities, projects, and long-term directions.

Reasons can conflict and need not share one unit.

## 5. When reasons do not uniquely determine the choice

The system can use an explicit choice procedure, such as:

- reflective commitment among supported paths;
- previously accepted priorities or conventions;
- fair allocation or rotation;
- negotiation with affected participants;
- random tie-breaking among genuinely symmetric options;
- delay when delay itself remains acceptable;
- choosing greater future maneuverability when that consideration is relevant.

The choice procedure itself is part of the decision basis.

`chosen != proven uniquely superior`

`random tie-break != repair missing sufficiency`

`preference != authority`

## 6. Decision creates commitment and path dependence

A decision can close alternatives, allocate resources, create expectations, trigger authorization, and change future reachability.

A consequential decision should preserve where practical:

- boundary version;
- candidate set;
- important exclusions and grounds;
- remaining options;
- important reasons and conflicts;
- accepted unknowns;
- selected option;
- decision-maker and decision authority;
- commitments created;
- expected effects;
- review, reversal, and reopening conditions;
- decision time.

Then it enters the [Action chain](../lifecycle.md) for authorization, execution, and reality-side verification.

## 7. Responsibility under uncertainty

Accountability does not require pretending that all consequences were knowable in advance.

A decision can carry proportionate responsibility for:

- evidence and reasons actually available at the time;
- important unknowns knowingly accepted;
- reasonably foreseeable effects and affected parties;
- whether safer or more recoverable paths existed;
- monitoring important reality-side effects;
- correction, compensation, stopping, or reopening when material deviation appears;
- not rewriting historical grounds after the outcome is known.

`bad outcome != earlier decision necessarily unreasonable`

`good outcome != decision process necessarily sound`

`acknowledged uncertainty != no responsibility`

## 8. Multi-participant decision

At minimum distinguish:

- who defines candidates;
- who provides evidence;
- who judges sufficiency;
- who has formal decision power;
- who is affected;
- whose consent, approval, or representation is necessary;
- who can appeal, veto, exit, or reopen;
- who bears execution and recovery responsibility.

`individual preference != collective value`

`aggregate preference != legitimate collective decision`

`strategic advantage != decision authority`

`decision authority != legitimacy`

Collective procedures need not discover a hidden unique optimum. They may instead make power, representation, rights, disagreement, and revision manageable and inspectable.

## 9. Reopening a decision

The original sufficiency basis can fail when:

- important facts or assumptions change;
- the current boundary proves insufficient;
- relations, power, or affected parties change;
- causal assumptions are falsified;
- capability, authority, dependencies, or recovery conditions change;
- value commitments are revised;
- material rights or participants were omitted;
- reality-side effects materially diverge from expectations;
- lock-in forms faster than correction;
- previously inaccessible alternatives become reachable.

`reopen != automatic reversal`

`historical decision invalid now != historical decision never existed`

## 10. Common failures

- optimization laundering: hiding value choices in the objective and reporting the result as technical necessity;
- rule laundering: treating rule satisfaction as "therefore this action should be chosen";
- capability determinism: easiest or strongest available action becomes what ought to be done;
- authority collapse: expertise, information, or capability automatically creates decision power;
- arbitrariness disguised as freedom: multiple admissible options but no preserved reasons;
- false necessity: important alternatives remain yet choice is described as forced;
- outcome rewriting history;
- unnecessarily irreversible commitment.

The goal is neither maximum optimization nor maximum arbitrariness, but **bounded, attributable, revisable choice**.

## 11. Keep evaluation, prediction, and choice distinct

Evaluation assesses an object under a specified criterion; prediction concerns an outcome under stated conditions and a time horizon; choice also depends on admissible options, values, risks, responsibility, and decision authority. A unique mathematical solution need not imply a unique warranted real-world action. Predictive performance cannot silently override rights, consent, or authorization.

When evaluations or forecasts materially shape a decision, retain the criterion, model version, significant unknowns, proportionate verification grounds, and reopening triggers. Evaluate external outcomes when they become observable. See the [judgment and prediction validation protocol](../../use/evaluation.md) and [prediction cases](../../use/prediction.md) and [mechanism cases](../../use/interaction.md).
