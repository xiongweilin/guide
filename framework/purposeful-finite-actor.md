# Bounded actor: controller first, reflexive identity optional

[English](purposeful-finite-actor.md) | [简体中文](purposeful-finite-actor.zh-CN.md)

The working subject of guide's **core action theory** is a controller with an external world, bounded actionable information, available action, feedback and externally supplied purposes or constraints. It may be a person, service, institution or a simple control loop; it need not describe itself as a self.

## Minimal capabilities

1. **Representable uncertainty:** retained observation/history constrains possible realities without assuming completeness.
2. **Goal-relevant action:** available transitions affect possible future outcomes, subject to actual authority and resource constraints.
3. **Finite effective resources:** time, observation, computation, interfaces, recovery and human assistance may be bounded; the total state space need not be mathematically finite.
4. **Feedback:** outcomes may be observed, but the observer may be stale or wrong. Unknown effects remain unknown.
5. **Action relevance:** some continuations are permitted, preferred or forbidden; no scalar utility is required.

Distinguish *possible*, *reachable*, *controllable*, *callable* and *authorized*. None implies the next. Local grounds for one action do not establish authority to perform it.

## Optional reflexive actor

An enriched actor can additionally retain a self/non-self distinction, model its own capability, maintain identity continuity, and revise that self-model after mismatch. These are useful for longitudinal agency, self-improvement and institutional identity, **not universal prerequisites of action**.

Even with reflexivity, subjective experience, personhood, legitimacy, rights and complete self-knowledge remain unproved. An actor's termination does not erase effects already committed to reality.

For common safe decisions and branching evidence policies, use [the action-first derivation](../minimal-derivation.md). For temporal continuation, use [lifecycle](lifecycle.md).
