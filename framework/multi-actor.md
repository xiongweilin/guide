# Multiple purposeful finite actors

[English](./multi-actor.md) | [简体中文](./multi-actor.zh-CN.md)

Multiple purposeful finite actors can coexist in external reality. Shared reality does not imply shared representation, purpose, or authority.

## 1. One reality, different local boundaries

Each actor has its own bounded representation map `qi: HR → Mi`, so generally:

`Bi ≠ Bj`

Differences may come from sensing and data, tools and interfaces, language and classification, history, timescale, purpose and constraints, permissions, or resources.

Using the same words does not guarantee the same boundary.

## 2. Actors affect one another through reality

One actor's action can become part of the later real conditions faced by another:

`Ai → Reality → Aj`

Multi-actor systems therefore naturally contain information externalities, resource competition, cooperation, commitment, adaptation, strategic behavior, feedback, and reflexivity.

## 3. A model of another actor remains a model

An actor may form a working representation of what another actor knows, seeks, can do, is constrained by, or may do next.

But:

`my model of another actor ≠ that actor's internal reality`

Infinite higher-order “I think you think I think...” reasoning is not a general requirement. Add another level only when it materially changes current action.

## 4. Coordination does not require complete representational unification

Coordination may rely on:

- shared partial facts;
- common distinctions;
- explicit translation across representations;
- agreement only on interfaces, commitments, or outcomes;
- action coordination despite theoretical or value disagreement.

`coordination possible ≠ complete shared worldview required`

## 5. Conflict has different sources

At minimum distinguish conflict in facts / evidence, classification and framing, causal mechanism, timescale, resources and reachability, purpose / value, and authority / institution.

These cannot be silently substituted by one generic disagreement label.

## 6. Multiple actors do not by themselves derive governance norms

The existence of multiple actors does not determine who should decide, who has rights, which procedure is legitimate, whether majorities are final, or whether total utility should be maximized.

Those require additional normative, institutional, and historical grounds.

`strategic advantage ≠ authority ≠ legitimacy`

## 7. Collective actors

Teams, organizations, and institutions can themselves be treated as purposeful finite actors at an appropriate level if they have persistent state, an identifiable influence boundary, some purpose or constraint, and action paths that change reality.

But the collective boundary is not automatically the sum of member boundaries:

`Bcollective ≠ Σ Bi`

Internal information, incentives, roles, permissions, and conflict can change collective behavior.
