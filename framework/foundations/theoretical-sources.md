# Theoretical sources and neighboring traditions

[English](./theoretical-sources.md) | [简体中文](./theoretical-sources.zh-CN.md)

guide draws on several major traditions while preserving explicit boundaries between them. It does not claim an authoritative genealogy or a deduction from any single tradition.

## 1. Bounded rationality

Herbert A. Simon's bounded rationality and satisficing are among the most direct backgrounds: real actors face limits of information, computation, time, and search cost, so exhaustive global optimization cannot be a general precondition for action.

Shared structure includes:

- finitude as the normal case;
- a need to stop search;
- “adequate for the next step” as distinct from a complete optimum.

guide extends sufficiency beyond candidate choice to framing, evidence, transition admission, verification, and reopening.

## 2. Pragmatism and fallibilism

Pragmatist and fallibilist traditions associated with Peirce, James, Dewey, and others emphasize inquiry through practice, revisability by consequences, and action without requiring absolute certainty.

guide is close to these traditions in holding that:

- current representation is not reality itself;
- provisional closure can support action;
- real consequences can reopen old boundaries;
- exploration should increase discrimination rather than merely accumulate description.

guide does not require any one pragmatist theory of truth.

## 3. Cybernetics, systems, and feedback

Cybernetic traditions associated with Wiener, Ashby, and others center system-environment coupling, feedback, regulation, stability, and bounded control.

Shared structure includes bidirectional actor-reality coupling, feedback changing later state, control bounded by interfaces and resources, and structural importance of stability, recovery, correction rate, and loss of control.

guide does not assume that all reality is one control system or that controllability implies that control is normatively justified.

## 4. Decision theory, control theory, and partial observability

Decision theory, control theory, MDPs, POMDPs, and neighboring formal frameworks provide useful local resources: state, observation, action, partial observability, action-sensitive successor states, policy, cost, risk, and value of information.

guide differs by not assuming that the state space, candidate space, utility function, probability model, or observation model has already been adequately specified. Those structures may themselves require exploration and revision.

## 5. Scientific method, falsification, and causal inference

Scientific practice, Popperian falsification, and modern causal inference contribute competing explanations, counterexamples, distinction between observation and intervention, proportional claim and evidence strength, and extra grounds for cross-environment transfer.

guide prescribes no single scientific method or causal formalism.

## 6. Multi-agent, game, organization, and institution theories

Game theory, multi-agent systems, organization theory, and institutional analysis make visible that actors differ in information and purpose, one actor changes conditions faced by others, coordination and conflict feed back into reality, and organizations cannot always be treated as enlarged individuals.

guide incorporates these structural issues without deriving a particular political, economic, or governance theory from the mere existence of multiple actors.

## 7. Affinity with phenomenology, epistemology, and perspectivism

guide shares a minimum intuition with phenomenology, perspectivism, and several epistemological traditions: actors form representations from local conditions, and the currently experienceable or distinguishable range is not identical to reality as a whole.

guide does not begin from first-person experience and does not turn conditions of knowledge into an ultimate ontology.

## 8. Process and lifecycle perspectives

Process philosophy, developmental systems, lifecycle engineering, and long-running systems research all emphasize that static snapshots are insufficient: path, persistence, transition, termination, and residual effects matter.

guide uses a process perspective for the finite lifetime of purposeful actors without claiming that process is the only ultimate ontology.

## 9. Normative theory

Ethics, philosophy of law, political philosophy, and normative decision theory provide many competing accounts of value, rights, commitment, public norms, and legitimacy.

guide selects no ultimate normative source. It only requires:

`fact / capability ≠ value / legitimacy`

and that material normative differences not be silently hidden inside technical objectives or optimization functions.

## 10. guide's combined position

A rough source map is:

`bounded rationality + fallibilism + feedback / control + partially observable decision + multi-actor interaction + non-substitution of normative semantics`

This map is not a proof.

guide's distinctive compression is to ask one shared question across these traditions:

> How does a purposeful finite actor in external reality form a working boundary that is provisionally sufficient for a current purpose and next transition, while remaining reopenable by reality?
