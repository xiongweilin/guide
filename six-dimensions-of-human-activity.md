# Six Basic Dimensions of Human Activity

This repository does not collapse knowledge, value, action, change, and social relations into one problem. For finite human beings and the teams, organizations, institutions, and technical systems through which human activity is extended, at least six basic dimensions must be kept distinct:

`distinction / qualification / value / capability / change / others`

These are not six kinds of object or six isolated modules. They are six coordinates that should remain available when examining the same human activity. A concrete activity usually has a position on all six. What holds in one dimension does not silently substitute for what must hold in another.

The six dimensions describe **human activity within a distinguishable world**. They are not a final ontology of the World itself. The World remains a cognitive boundary.

## 1. Distinction: what enters the current world

Basic structure:

`World (cognitive boundary) → distinguishable world / distinguishable range → distinguished range → current representation`

- **World (cognitive boundary)**: current observation, language, models, and classifications are not entitled by default to exhaust the World itself.
- **Distinguishable world / distinguishable range**: distinctions that could in principle be formed under current sensing, language, tools, interaction, capability, and interface conditions.
- **Distinguished range**: distinctions actually acquired, formed, and currently invocable.
- **Current representation**: objects, concepts, relations, structures, and judgments further organized from the distinguished range.

This dimension first asks:

> What has actually been distinguished? What remains distinguishable but not yet formed? Which boundaries are only defaults?

Language, culture, interfaces, historical decisions, and existing models provide many **default distinctions**. Being default does not make a distinction correct or make it a boundary of the World itself.

## 2. Qualification: what is sufficient to enter the next state

Minimal state structure:

`unmet → accumulating → sufficient → enter`

Qualification does not primarily ask what something is. It asks:

> Under the current scope, purpose, time scale, and conditions, what makes a candidate or state sufficient to remain, advance, close, reopen, or exit?

“Accumulating” need not mean numerical accumulation, and “sufficient” need not mean one scalar threshold. Qualification may depend on:

- all necessary conditions being met;
- sufficient evidential support;
- competing explanations being distinguished;
- authority, approval, or commitment holding;
- verification being established;
- risk, cost, and residual unknowns being acceptable for the current purpose.

Qualification is sliced:

`question qualification != judgment qualification != decision qualification != action qualification != completion qualification != long-term validity`

Validity in one slice does not silently carry into the next.

## 3. Value: what is worth pursuing, required, or rejected

Basic tension:

`external norms ↔ internal norms`

Human activity does not only ask what is true. It also asks:

> What is worth pursuing, avoiding, maintaining, committing to, or refusing?

External norms may come from family, culture, law, institutions, roles, organizations, markets, and relationships. Internal norms may come from experience, preference, reflection, identity, and active commitment.

The two are not a simple binary. External norms can be internalized until their source is no longer noticed; internal norms can also change through reflection. Keep separate:

- where a norm comes from;
- whether the current person recognizes its source;
- whether it has reflective endorsement;
- whether it has authority over others;
- whether public legitimacy is required.

Explaining where a value came from does not automatically validate or invalidate it. Personal endorsement also does not automatically create authority over others.

## 4. Capability: what can actually be done

Basic expansion structure:

`cannot → self-capable → assisted-capable → leverage-capable`

- **Cannot**: no currently available feasible path.
- **Self-capable**: achievable through one's existing body, knowledge, skills, and directly controlled resources.
- **Assisted-capable**: achievable through tools, devices, AI, institutional interfaces, professional services, or other external capability.
- **Leverage-capable**: achievable without personally carrying the main causal process, by using environment, rules, networks, incentives, cooperation, automation, or already existing trends.

This dimension asks:

> Which causal structures can the current participant invoke, and which states does that make reachable?

Capability is not permission and is not value:

`can do != authorized to do != worth doing`

Much of modern human capability is distributed across tools, infrastructure, organizations, relationships, and institutions rather than stored in one person's body or mind.

## 5. Change: whether the current structure is stable

Basic structure:

`stable / transitional / no stable structure`

- **Stable**: within the relevant time scale and disturbance range, the structure persists enough to serve as a working object.
- **Transitional**: the present form is mainly a process from one organization toward another; direction, rate, path, and transition conditions matter more than static classification.
- **No stable structure**: at the current scale there is not enough persistence for a reliable fixed model; forcing stable boundaries may manufacture a false object.

This dimension asks:

> Are we observing a persistent structure, a transition process, or a situation in which no stable structure has yet formed?

Stable does not mean permanently unchanged. Transitional does not mean the destination is already known. Every working structure needs a scope, time scale, and reassessment condition.

## 6. Others: how multiple participants are coupled

Minimal coordinates:

`relation strength × alignment / conflict`

- **Relation strength**: how strongly one participant's state, choice, or action changes another participant's reachable paths, information, value, cost, or outcome.
- **Alignment / conflict**: whether participants are compatible, cooperative, competitive, or mutually excluding in the relevant dimension.

Alignment and conflict are not one total label. Participants may have:

- aligned factual judgments but conflicting values;
- aligned values but conflicting methods;
- aligned goals but competing for resources;
- short-term alignment and long-term conflict;
- strong coupling in one scope and almost no relation in another.

Multi-participant activity therefore also requires distinctions among information, classifications, values, capabilities, authority, commitment, recognition, and legitimacy.

## 7. The six dimensions are not six unrelated lines

The dimensions can change one another, but they cannot substitute for one another.

For example:

- a new **distinction** can reframe what looked like a value conflict;
- new evidence can change the **qualification** of a judgment;
- **value** supplies direction among possible states;
- **capability** determines which of those states are currently reachable;
- **change** determines how long current categories and rules can remain usable;
- **others** couple individual distinctions, values, and capabilities into coordination, competition, commitment, and institutions.

A minimal human-activity check can therefore ask:

1. **Distinction**: what are we currently treating as the objects, boundaries, and problem?
2. **Qualification**: what is currently sufficient to enter the next state?
3. **Value**: which directions are required, preferred, or committed to, and where do those norms come from?
4. **Capability**: what is currently unreachable, self-reachable, reachable with assistance, or reachable through leverage?
5. **Change**: are the relevant structures stable, transitional, or not yet stable?
6. **Others**: who is strongly coupled to whom, and in which dimensions are they aligned or in conflict?
