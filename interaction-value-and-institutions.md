# Interaction, Value, and Institutions

In multi-agent environments, facts, models, goals, permissions, and values may differ. We cannot assume that all participants share one complete and internally consistent view of the "real situation."

## 1. Strategic agents and local representations

Strategic interaction exists whenever one agent's judgment or action changes another agent's information, reality, judgment, or reachable paths.

An agent may be an individual, team, organization, institution, or technical system. Being a strategic agent does not automatically imply subjective experience, moral responsibility, or formal authority.

Each agent may have only a local representation and maintain only limited-depth beliefs about others, such as "I think this is what you think," rather than infinite recursion. Structural unknowns can also include missing participants, actions, information, timing, and rules.

## 2. Behavior, signals, and evidence are different

An action occurring does not mean an observer has acquired it; acquiring it does not mean it has been interpreted correctly; interpreting it does not mean it provides enough support for a conclusion.

Therefore distinguish:

`behavior → observed signal → current interpretation → evidential support strength → judgment`

Queries, probes, or experiments can help distinguish competing models, but information value does not automatically create action authority.

## 3. Models can change what they model

In strategic environments, rules, predictions, public models, and decisions can themselves change participant behavior.

Publishing a forecast, changing rewards, setting metrics, or changing review rules can alter the subsequent data-generating process.

Old models, metrics, and judgments therefore need to be reexamined after the environment changes.

## 4. Formal authority, factual recognition, and legitimacy must remain distinct

- **Formal authority**: who has decision rights under the current institution;
- **Factual recognition**: whether other agents actually recognize that authority or identity;
- **Legitimacy**: whether the authority or rule has sufficient normative basis.

Strategic advantage does not create formal authority; formal authority does not guarantee recognition; recognition does not prove the institution itself is legitimate.

## 5. Value has at least four layers

### Functional layer

A system tends to approach, avoid, maintain, or regulate certain outcomes.

This can be represented by reward signals, control objectives, or mechanisms that preserve internal stability, but it does not automatically imply subjective experience or normative value.

### Experiential layer

The question becomes whether subjective experience exists and whether that experience has positive or negative valence.

Functional complexity alone cannot answer this layer.

### Reflective layer

An agent may reflect on its own preferences, identity, commitments, and direction of life and form reflective endorsement.

Personal endorsement does not automatically create authority over others.

### Public normative layer

When decisions affect other people, rights, shared resources, third parties, or institutions, additional public norms and legitimacy judgments are required.

Every move across layers requires new evidence and qualification; higher layers cannot be inferred automatically from lower ones.

## 6. Subjective experience requires a separate judgment

None of the following facts alone is sufficient to prove subjective experience:

- self-maintenance;
- reward signals;
- goal representation;
- complex behavior;
- self-models;
- language;
- learning and adaptation;
- strategic behavior.

If a mistaken judgment could cause irreversible suffering or other experiential harm, precautionary measures may be appropriate; precaution does not mean subjective experience has already been proven.

## 7. The source of a value and the validity of a value are different

A value can arise from the body, experience, relationships, education, institutions, habits, path dependence, trauma, loss, reflective endorsement, or rejection.

Explaining where a value came from does not automatically invalidate it; having a source does not automatically prove that it should bind others.

## 8. Commitments need conditions of freedom and conditions for reopening

Coercion, manipulation, lack of an exit path, or severe dependency can weaken a "surface commitment" as evidence of a free and stable commitment.

Changes that commonly require a value or institutional arrangement to be reconsidered include:

- persistent incompatibility;
- the factual basis being overturned;
- major changes in external costs;
- changes in participants or boundaries;
- the appearance of coercion or manipulation;
- loss of exit capability;
- major changes in dependency relations.

Reconsideration does not automatically reveal a new "true value." It only means the old qualification is no longer sufficient to remain valid by default.

## 9. Qualification conflict is one of the core forms of institutional conflict

Multi-agent conflicts are often not just disputes about facts. They are disputes over:

- whose evidence enters a decision;
- who can define the rules;
- who can require the current dispute to end;
- who can require renewed review;
- which commitments remain valid;
- which losses must enter the constraints;
- which authority should be recognized;
- why the rules themselves should continue to hold.

The task of an institution is not to eliminate difference. It is to provide inspectable procedures for decision, review, exit, and amendment when these conflicts occur.
